# Dremel Guide for 3D Printing Post-Processing (PLA / PLA+)

A practical reference for cleaning up and finishing FDM prints with a rotary tool. Tuned for PLA and PLA+, which are soft and heat-sensitive — most of the technique here is about *not* melting your part.

## Quick Reference

| Material | Starting RPM | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| PLA / PLA+ | 5,000–10,000 | Heat-sensitive; light touch, low speed |
| PETG | 10,000–15,000 | Slightly more heat tolerance |
| ABS / ASA | 15,000–25,000 | Sands cleanly; can go faster |

> **Golden rule for PLA:** if you smell melting plastic or see shiny smearing, you're going too fast or pressing too hard. Back off both.

### Recommended Add-On: Flex Shaft (225-02)

A significant quality-of-life upgrade for 3D print work. It lets you hold the working end like a pen, giving you much finer control on small detailed parts and tight support-removal areas. Available bundled with the 4300-5/40 or separately.

> **Note:** the 225-02 flex shaft is rated to a maximum of 30,000 RPM — keep the tool at or below that when it's attached. Hang the motor body above the work (e.g., from a hook or stand) so the shaft isn't kinked; a tight bend wears the inner cable and adds drag.

## Kit Accessories & How to Use Them

### Sanding Drums & Bands

- **60-grit** — aggressive material removal; knocking down large support remnants and rough surface bumps
- **120-grit** — intermediate smoothing after the 60-grit pass
- **240-grit** — final smoothing pass before polishing

> **Tip:** work through the grits in order — don't skip steps. Jumping from 60 straight to 240 leaves deep scratches that show up clearly after polishing or painting.

### Aluminum Oxide Grinding Stones

- **5/8" stone** — general grinding on larger flat or curved surfaces
- **3/8" stone** — mid-size areas and moderate detail work
- Good for tight corners where sanding bands can't reach, and for shaving down stubborn support nubs

### Silicon Carbide Grinding Stone (3/16")

- Harder and more aggressive than aluminum oxide
- Use for particularly rough spots or sharp points that need material removed quickly
- Best used *before* switching to the aluminum oxide stones for finer work

### Felt Polishing Wheels + Compound

- **1/2" wheels** — detail areas, small features, and tight spots
- **1" wheels** — larger flat areas and broader curved surfaces
- Use with the included polishing compound to restore the sheen sanding removes from PLA
- This is the final step — afterward the surface should look close to the original print finish

> **Caution:** polishing generates friction heat fast on PLA. Keep the speed low (5,000–10,000 RPM), keep the wheel moving, and don't dwell in one spot or you'll glaze/melt the surface.

## Pro Tips for PLA / PLA+

**Keep speed low.** PLA has a low melting temperature, so friction from a fast bit melts and smears the plastic instead of cutting it cleanly. Start at 5,000–10,000 RPM. Only go higher on harder materials like ABS or PETG.

**Use a light touch.** Let the accessory do the work. Excess pressure deforms thin walls, builds heat, and causes smearing. Light, controlled passes win every time.

**Keep the bit moving.** Heat is cumulative in one spot. Continuous motion spreads it out and prevents the localized melting that ruins a surface.

**Work in small sections.** Tackle small areas rather than running the tool across a whole face at once. Better control, and easier to check progress without oversanding.

**Use the flex shaft for detail work.** For intricate areas, small parts, or anywhere you need pen-like precision, hold the flex shaft like a pencil for far more control than gripping the full tool body.

**Follow up with hand sanding.** For the best final finish — especially before painting — progress through wet sanding by hand:

1. 150 grit
2. 220 grit
3. 400 grit
4. 600+ grit (wet — dip the paper in water to cut friction and heat)

Wet sanding matters most at the higher grits: it prevents heat buildup and gives a much smoother result.

**Restore the sheen after sanding.** PLA loses its shine when sanded. To bring the luster back, rub on a small amount of mineral/baby oil, or use the polishing compound with a felt wheel.

## Safety

- **Eye protection is non-negotiable.** Bits throw plastic chips and the occasional fragment at speed. Wear safety glasses every time.
- **Protect your lungs.** Sanding PLA makes fine plastic dust. Work in a ventilated area and wear an N95 / dust mask — the tool's airflow helps, but the dust still goes somewhere.
- **Secure the part, not your fingers.** Clamp the workpiece or use a vise where you can; keep hands clear of the spinning bit.
- **Tie back hair, lose loose sleeves.** A rotary tool grabs anything dangling.

## Suggested Workflow

1. **Remove supports by hand first** — flush cutters or pliers to get the bulk off before the Dremel touches it
2. **Silicon carbide stone** — knock down sharp nubs or high points
3. **Aluminum oxide stones** — refine tight corners and detail areas
4. **60-grit sanding drum** — smooth the broader support-scar areas (low speed, light touch)
5. **120-grit band** — intermediate smoothing pass
6. **240-grit band** — final sanding pass
7. **Hand wet sand** (optional, for best finish) — 400 then 600+ grit
8. **Felt wheel + polishing compound** — restore shine

> **Always test on a scrap print or a hidden area first** to dial in speed and pressure before touching a visible surface.
